# Prose voice

Write for a reader who has never heard of the person, who may be twelve or ninety, and who may only see one sentence
of the page, quoted by a search engine or an AI tool. Plain, exact and kind.

## The lead summary (answer first)

Every profile and story **opens with a lead summary**: two or three sentences that answer "Who was [Name]?" on their
own, before any background. It names the person, the life dates, the service or role, and the Monmouth connection, in
the wording the evidence grades allow.

> [Name] ([life dates]) was a private in [unit], [Monmouth connection]. [One sentence on what the records show the
> person did.] [One sentence on where the person rests or what is named for them, if known.]

**Life dates carry their grade too.** Write "c. 1745" or "about 1745" for a year worked out from an age, "bapt. 1745"
for a baptism rather than a birth, "1745?" or "probably born 1745" for a probable year, and leave a date out rather
than guess. An age taken from a pension declaration made decades later is rarely better than Probable.

If the grade for any part is below Proved, the summary says so in that grade's words ("probably", "may have"). A
summary that turns a probable fact into a certain one is wrong even if every later paragraph is careful.

## Other rules

- **Plain words.** Prefer "enlisted" to "was enrolled into the service of". Explain any period term once.
- **Grade wording everywhere.** Follow [`evidence-rules.md`](./evidence-rules.md): a plain statement claims Proved.
- **Question-shaped headings where natural:** "Was [Name] at the Battle of Monmouth?", "Where is [Name] buried?", "Why
  is the bridge named for [Name]?".
- **State hard facts plainly.** Where the records show that a patriot held people in slavery, say so, and name the
  enslaved people where the record names them. Do not soften it, explain it away, or move it to a footnote.
- **Weigh evidence against patriot status; do not hide it or prejudge it.** A court-martial, an oath or protection
  taken from the Crown, or a desertion is recorded as a finding, with its source and its context: in occupied
  Monmouth, protections were often taken under duress, and many deserters returned to service. The finding is weighed
  with the rest of the evidence; it is not an automatic disqualification.
- **No embellishment.** No invented dialogue, thoughts or weather. A quotation is a quotation from a record, cited.
- **Name what is not known.** Close each profile's narrative with what remains open, and link to the open questions.
- **Respect the people.** Black people, women and Native people are named and described as people, with their own
  records and their own service, not as background to someone else's. Do not assume a patriot was a man.
