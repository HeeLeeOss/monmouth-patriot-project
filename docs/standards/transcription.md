# Transcription conventions

A transcription records what the page says, not what it should have said. Anyone comparing it with the image should
be able to see exactly where we read, guessed or added something.

## The rules

- **Spelling as written.** Keep the original spelling, capitals, abbreviations and punctuation. "Monmouth" spelt
  "Monmoth" stays "Monmoth". Do not modernise, correct or expand silently.
- **Line breaks** follow the original. Mark a new page with `[page n]` or `[image n]`.
- **`[ ]` for editorial notes.** Anything we add goes in square brackets: `[torn]`, `[blot]`, `[margin: …]`,
  `[signature]`, `[sic]` (sparingly), or an expansion such as `Capt[ain]`.
- **`[?]` for an uncertain reading.** Put it straight after the doubtful word: `Shrewsb[?]ry` for a doubtful letter,
  `Holmdel [?]` for a doubtful word. Give alternatives where useful: `Hendrickson [or Hendricks?]`.
- **`[illegible]`** for a word that cannot be read, with the approximate length if known: `[illegible, 2 words]`.
- **Struck-through text** is kept and marked: `~~text~~`. Insertions above the line go in `^…^`.
- **Names and numbers** are copied exactly, including obvious errors; the correction goes in the profile, with its
  evidence, not in the transcription.

## Who checks it

Every transcription is **checked by a person against the page image** before it is cited, and that person is
recorded as `verifiedBy` with the date. This applies above all to OCR output and to any reading produced by an AI tool,
which can be fluent and wrong.

A transcription made only by a tool, and not yet checked, may guide research but cannot support a published claim.

Each transcription records `transcribedBy` (a person or the tool used) and `verifiedBy` (always a person).
